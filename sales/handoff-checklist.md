# Client handoff checklist

Give this to the client at launch, and use it if they ever leave. The goal: they can move on to another developer in one afternoon.

## Who owns what
| Asset | Owner | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name | Client (registered under their name and email) | You, with delegated access |
| Website files and code | You build it, client gets a full copy on request | You (private repo) |
| Hosting | You | You |
| Contact form service | You | You |
| Google Business Profile | Client stays Primary Owner | You as Manager |
| Their photos, text, logo, reviews | Client | n/a |

## Setup rules for every client
- Register the domain under the **client's** email and account. Write down the registrar and where the 2FA recovery goes.
- Never ask for or store a password in chat or text. Use a shared password manager vault.
- Turn on two-factor authentication on every account you create.
- Keep each client in a separate hosting project and a separate private repo.
- Don't collect payment details or health information on these sites.

## If they cancel (30 days' notice)
1. Send them a zip of the complete site files plus this checklist, free.
2. Ask where the site should go next (new host or new developer).
3. If they want you to move it: optional one-time migration fee.
4. Help point the domain's DNS to the new host. Confirm the new site works before changing anything.
5. After they confirm: remove your access (Google Business Profile manager, form service, any shared accounts) and delete the old hosting project.
6. Tell them what to hand to the next developer (below).

## What the next developer needs
- Domain registrar login and the current DNS records
- The site files (zip or repo access)
- The contact form service and the email address it sends to
- Google Business Profile and analytics access
- Any third-party logins you set up for them (booking, call tracking, AI assistant)
- This checklist and the list of edits you made

## When a client needs more than this
Say plainly: "That needs a different kind of site (online store, customer logins, a system they edit themselves, or anything handling payments or sensitive data), and I'd refer you to someone set up for it."
