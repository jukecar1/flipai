## Script

**Hook (0-3s)**
You think this is how gas stations make their money? Think again. The net profit margin on a gallon of gas is incredibly low, averaging just 1.4%. After expenses like credit card processing fees, rent, and labor, the owner might only pocket a few cents from your fill-up. So if the pump isn't the profit center, where's the real money?

**Setup / Payoff (3-45s)**
It's inside the convenience store. While fuel drives the most revenue, often accounting for about two-thirds of total sales, it's the in-store items that deliver the majority of the profits. High-margin products are the true engine of the business. Foodservice — things like prepared foods, coffee, and fountain drinks — is especially profitable, accounting for roughly 39% of in-store gross profit. Other items like snacks and drinks also have strong margins, turning a quick fuel stop into a much more valuable transaction.

Even the location itself is strategic. Stations are often built on high-traffic roads or busy intersections for maximum visibility and easy access. The whole model is designed to get you on the lot for gas, and then inside the door for everything else.

**Ending (45-60s)**
Follow for more business explained.
