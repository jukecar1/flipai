**Title:** What if every state's minimum wage kept up with inflation? 🗺️💵

**Caption:**
In 1968 the federal minimum wage was $1.60 an hour. If it had kept pace with prices, it would be $14.81 today. Instead it's $7.25, untouched since July 24, 2009. Here's every state, 1968 to 2025: the solid blocks are what the minimum really was, and the amber glass is what it would be if it had matched inflation. 😳

The numbers:
• Federal minimum wage history: U.S. Department of Labor ($1.60 in 1968, $3.35 in 1981, $5.15 in Sept 1997, $7.25 on July 24, 2009)
• State minimum wages: Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED) state series, from state and DOL records, as of Jan 1 of each year. Where a state has no minimum or a lower one, the federal rate applies. On Jan 1, 2025: 30 states and D.C. above $7.25, 20 states at the federal rate
• Inflation: CPI-U annual average (BLS via FRED). "If it matched inflation" = each state's 1968 minimum (the greater of its own rate and $1.60) × CPI(year) ÷ CPI(1968). Most states land at $14.81. Alaska ($2.10 in 1968) would be $19.44 and California ($1.65) $15.27. This is my arithmetic, starting from 1968, the year the federal minimum's buying power peaked
• Only 13 of 51 places (12 states and D.C.) are at or above their own indexed rate on Jan 1, 2025: CA, CO, CT, DE, IL, MD, MA, NJ, NY, OR, RI, WA and D.C.
• Highest on Jan 1, 2025: D.C. $17.50, Washington $16.66, California and New York $16.50
• Economists disagree about the effects. CBO's 2021 estimate for a $15 federal minimum by 2025: about 1.4 million fewer jobs and about 900,000 fewer people in poverty
