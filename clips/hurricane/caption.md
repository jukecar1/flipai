**Title:** What if a Category 5 hurricane hit Miami? 🌀

**Caption:**
You're watching from the 3rd floor as the wind climbs past 157 mph, the window blows in, and the sea comes up the street. Then the eye passes and everything goes quiet, until the other wall hits. 😳

This is time-compressed and illustrative. Real storms play out over many hours.

The numbers:
• Category 5 = sustained winds of 157 mph or more (Saffir-Simpson scale)
• Hurricane Andrew hit Miami-Dade in 1992 at about 165 mph (NOAA reanalysis made it a Category 5)
• Andrew's storm surge peaked at 16.9 ft in Biscayne Bay (the video's surge peaks at about 17 ft)
• Many Miami streets sit only about 6 ft above sea level, so a surge that size puts roughly 11 ft of water in the street
• Wind pressure on a wall at 160 mph: about 65 lb per sq ft (my estimate, ½ × air density × speed squared)
• Storm surge caused 49% of direct U.S. hurricane deaths from 1963 to 2012 (NHC)
