# Sources and assumptions: "What if you drove to the Moon?"

| Claim in the video | Basis |
|---|---|
| Moon is 238,855 miles away (average, center to center); range about 225,600 to 252,000 miles | Standard figure for the mean Earth-Moon distance (384,400 km); perigee about 363,300 km, apogee about 405,500 km (NASA / common references) |
| 153 days at 65 mph; 306 days round trip; 83 days at 120 mph; 110 days at 90 mph | **My arithmetic:** 238,855 mi ÷ (speed × 24 h) |
| Gas about 9,550 gal, about $30,000 | **My estimate:** 238,855 mi ÷ 25 mpg = 9,554 gal; × $3.10 per gal ≈ $29,600. Mileage and fuel price are assumptions |
| ISS about 250 miles up, GPS satellites about 12,550 miles, geostationary orbit about 22,236 miles, edge of space about 62 miles (Kármán line, 100 km) | Standard orbital altitudes (NASA / ESA). The video marks the odometer reading when the road passes each one |
| Tsunami more than 500 mph in the deep ocean, about 20 to 30 mph at the shore | NOAA tsunami physics pages: "in the deep ocean, tsunamis can move as fast as a jet plane, over 500 mph", and "at the shore, most tsunamis slow to the speed of a car, approximately 20 to 30 mph" |
| The sea pulling back before a tsunami; go uphill right away after strong shaking | Standard NOAA / National Weather Service tsunami safety guidance (natural warning signs: strong or long shaking, sea level changes) |

Illustrative (not data): the road to the Moon, the car, the coastal highway and beach, the size and timing of the wave (14 m high; time compressed), the "are we there yet?" counter, and the scenery on the Moon. The video is labeled "Illustrative · time compressed".

Notes: 238,855 miles is a center-to-center distance. Surface to surface it is about 233,800 miles (about 3% less), which the video ignores to keep the numbers simple.
