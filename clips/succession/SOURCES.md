# Sources and assumptions: "What happens in the first hours after a president is assassinated?"

Framing: an explainer about the office and the law. The video names no real person, shows no people and no attack. The vice president, Speaker and others are referred to by office only, because officeholders change.

| Claim in the video | Basis |
|---|---|
| The Vice President becomes President on the president's death | U.S. Constitution Art. II, §1, cl. 6 and the 25th Amendment, §1 |
| The oath is customary, usually within hours, and can happen anywhere | Historical practice (e.g. Johnson aboard Air Force One, 1963). The Constitution does not make the oath a condition of taking the office's powers |
| Kennedy pronounced dead 1:00 pm CST, Johnson sworn in 2:38 pm CST (1 h 38 min) | American Presidency Project; Warren Commission report. Swearing-in by Judge Sarah T. Hughes aboard Air Force One at Love Field, Dallas |
| NYSE closed early at 2:07 pm ET on Nov 22, 1963; closed Nov 25 for the funeral; reopened Nov 26, Dow +4.5% (about 32 points) | Market histories (TradingHours, Wikipedia "Reactions to the assassination of John F. Kennedy", JFK Library). The Friday drop is about 2.9% for the Dow in some sources and about 2.8% for the S&P 500 in others, so the video says "closed early" and uses the Dow |
| Flags at half-staff for 30 days after the death of a president | 4 U.S.C. § 7 (flag half-staff provisions) |
| New president nominates a Vice President, confirmed by a majority of both houses | 25th Amendment, §2. Used for Gerald Ford (1973) and Nelson Rockefeller (1974) |
| Line of succession: Vice President, Speaker, President pro tempore, then the Cabinet | Presidential Succession Act of 1947 (3 U.S.C. § 19) |
| Eight presidents died in office, four assassinated (Lincoln, Garfield, McKinley, Kennedy) | Standard presidential history |
| The president "lies in state at the Capitol" | Customary for presidents who die in office, authorized by Congress each time. Stated as the usual practice |
| The Secret Service moves the Vice President to a secure location | Standard protective practice. The video does not claim specific procedures |

Illustrative (not data): the scenery (a stylized Washington Mall, Monument, Capitol and White House), the motorcade, the timing of each shot, and the compressed day-to-month passage of time. The scene is labeled "Hypothetical · based on law and precedent".
