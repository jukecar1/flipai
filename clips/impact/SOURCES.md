# Sources for "What if the dinosaur-killing asteroid hit today?"

Scenario: a Chicxulub-size impact at the original site (Yucatan, Mexico); the viewer is walking in Manhattan, ~2,600 km (1,600 mi) away (great-circle distance computed from coordinates).

| On-screen claim | Source / basis |
|---|---|
| 66 million years ago, ~10 km asteroid hit what is now Mexico | Chicxulub impact; impactor ~10 km at ~20 km/s (Planetary Science Institute impact-crater simulations, psi.edu; NASA NTRS 19940023798) |
| 10 km is wider than Everest is tall | Everest = 8.85 km |
| ~20 km/s = about 45,000 mph | unit conversion (20 km/s = 44,700 mph) |
| Impact "in 0:07" countdown | ~7 s for a 20 km/s body to cross ~140 km of atmosphere at 45 degrees (calculation, shown slightly slower) |
| Energy ~100 million megatons; ~10,000x all nuclear arsenals | order-of-magnitude figures cited from the above sources |
| Seismic waves arrive in ~5:25; P-waves ~8 km/s; S-waves ~4 min later | calculation: 2,597 km / 8 km/s = 325 s; S-waves ~4.5 km/s -> 577 s |
| You would not see the impact | Earth's curvature hides it: at 2,600 km the surface drops ~530 km below the line of sight (d^2 / 2R) |
| Equivalent magnitude 9.4 to 11.3; record 9.5 (Chile 1960) | Bermudez et al. (Chicxulub "mega-earthquake"), reported by Space.com / LiveScience / Phys.org 2022; USGS for the 1960 record |
| Sky heat ~6 kW/m2 peak, about 6x midday sun; sky glows red; global fires | Melosh, Schneider, Zahnle, Latham, Nature 343, 251-254 (1990); later modelling notes self-shielding limits the pulse |
| Ejecta falls back through the sky | same as above (ejecta reentry); debris cloud circled Earth within hours |
| Gulf tsunami 300+ m open-ocean ~1 hour after impact | Range et al., AGU Advances (2022), AGU press release |
| Dark and cold for about a decade; ~75% of species lost | K-Pg extinction literature (e.g. Hildebrand; Toon et al. 1997) |
| No known asteroid this size threatens Earth in the next century | NASA Planetary Defense: ~95% of near-Earth asteroids >1 km found, none a known threat for 100+ years |

Not claimed as measured: the exact look of the plume, the damage to individual buildings, and the arrival time of the heat pulse at 1,100 km. These are stylized/illustrative.

Manhattan-specific caveat: how much damage 2,600 km of distance still allows is not well measured. The shaking, falling glass and masonry, cracks and fires shown are illustrative; the magnitude, heat-pulse and tsunami figures are the sourced ones.
