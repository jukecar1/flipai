# Sources and assumptions: "What if the federal minimum wage matched inflation every year?"

| Claim in the video | Basis |
|---|---|
| Federal minimum wage history: $1.60 (1968), $2.00 (May 1974), $2.10 (1975), $2.30 (1976), $2.65 (1978), $2.90 (1979), $3.10 (1980), $3.35 (Jan 1981), $3.80 (Apr 1990), $4.25 (Apr 1991), $4.75 (Oct 1996), $5.15 (Sep 1997), $5.85 (Jul 2007), $6.55 (Jul 2008), $7.25 (Jul 24, 2009) | U.S. Department of Labor, Wage and Hour Division, "History of Federal Minimum Wage Rates" (dol.gov/agencies/whd/minimum-wage/history). Cross-checked with the FRED series STTMINWGFG (1968 to 2026). The 1968 start is the $1.60 rate |
| Inflation | Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, not seasonally adjusted, annual average (BLS series CPIAUCNS via FRED). 1968: 34.8, 1981: 90.9, 1990: 130.7, 1997: 160.5, 2009: 214.5, 2025: 321.9 |
| "Indexed" wage: $14.81 in 2025 and the line over time | **My arithmetic:** $1.60 × CPI(year) ÷ CPI(1968), placed at the middle of each year and joined with straight lines. It is one scenario (indexing from the 1968 rate, when the real value was highest). Indexing from the original 1938 $0.25 gives about $5.71 in 2025, below the actual $7.25, so the starting point matters |
| $7.25 buys about half of 1968 buying power; $0.78 in 1968 dollars | **My arithmetic:** $7.25 × CPI(1968) ÷ CPI(2025) = $0.78; ÷ $1.60 = 49% |
| Full-time pay: $15,080 and about $30,800 | **My arithmetic:** hourly rate × 2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks) |
| 2025 poverty guidelines: $15,650 for one person, $26,650 for a family of three | U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (ASPE), 2025 poverty guidelines for the 48 contiguous states and D.C. (published Jan 2025). Shown for comparison only. The guidelines are not a budget or a measure of local cost of living |
| About $154,000 | **My arithmetic:** for each year 2010 to 2025, (indexed minus actual average wage) × 2,080 hours, added up in each year's dollars (not adjusted to today's dollars). That averages about $9,600 a year |
| Last raise July 24, 2009; no automatic increases | DOL wage history. Congress sets the federal rate by statute |
| 30 states and D.C. have a higher minimum than the federal rate | State minimum wage tallies for 2025 (DOL / NCSL-based summaries). Counts vary a little by source and date |
| CBO 2021: $15 by 2025 would mean about 1.4 million fewer jobs and about 900,000 fewer people in poverty | Congressional Budget Office, "The Budgetary Effects of the Raise the Wage Act of 2021" (Feb 2021, cbo.gov/publication/56975). Estimates for that specific bill, shown to note that the effects are debated |

The scenario is a thought experiment, not a forecast. It doesn't model how employers, prices or jobs would have responded.
