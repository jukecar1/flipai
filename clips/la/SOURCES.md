# Sources and assumptions: "Evolution of Los Angeles: 3D city timelapse (1781–2026)"

**What is real:** the terrain (AWS/Mapzen terrain tiles built from NASA SRTM and other elevation data); inside roughly lon -118.5 to -118.0, lat 33.7 to 34.2 (downtown, Hollywood, the Westside, South LA, the harbor area) the present-day street network, building footprints and heights, parks, industrial and retail zones, rivers and lakes, railways and LAX runways, all from OpenStreetMap (ODbL, BBBike extract of Oct 2026); the freeway routes elsewhere (Natural Earth 1:10m roads); and the milestone dates and population figures below.

**What is stylized:** *when* things appeared. The map shows today's streets and buildings, and each one is made to appear in a modeled year. Growth fronts start from founding nuclei (the Pueblo, Pasadena, Santa Monica, Long Beach, Hollywood, Glendale, Burbank, San Fernando, Anaheim and others), spread across the flat developable land, and are scaled to a plausible built-up-area curve, so individual construction dates are approximate. Tall buildings are placed by height era; the downtown towers with known dates (for example Aon Center 1973, U.S. Bank Tower 1989, Wilshire Grand 2017) use those years. About a third of the smaller buildings are drawn, to keep the video manageable. Outside the OpenStreetMap box (San Fernando Valley, Pasadena, Orange County) buildings are generic blocks. The 1781 pueblo is a few generic adobe boxes. Freeways are drawn outward from downtown across their decades (the Arroyo Seco Parkway in 1940, most others between the 1950s and 1970s, the I-105 in the late 1980s to 1993). Metro rail lines are drawn from 1990 outward. The Hollywood sign letters are placed from the known location. Building heights are exaggerated about 4.8×, and the vertical scale of the terrain about 3.2×.
