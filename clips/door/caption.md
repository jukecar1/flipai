**Title:** What if you opened a plane door mid-flight? ✈️

**Caption:**
You can't. At cruise, the air inside the cabin pushes on a door with roughly 15,000 lb of force, and the door is built to be pulled tighter by that pressure. 😳

So we also showed what happens if a door-sized hole opened up at 35,000 ft: the cabin pressure drops in seconds, the masks fall, and you have about 15–30 seconds of clear thinking. The scenes after the cut are illustrative, not a real incident.

Numbers (my estimates are marked):
• Cabin about 10.9 psi vs about 3.5 psi outside at 35,000 ft → about 7.4 psi difference (standard atmosphere, typical 8,000 ft cabin altitude)
• Force on a 30 × 70 in door: about 15,500 lb (my estimate)
• Useful consciousness at 35,000 ft: 30–60 s, about 15–30 s after a rapid decompression (FAA AC 61-107B)
• Real case: Alaska 1282 (Jan 2024), a door plug (not a passenger door) blew out at about 15,000 ft after its bolts were left out. No deaths (NTSB)
