# Sources and assumptions: "What if you opened a plane door mid-flight?"

| Claim in the video | Basis |
|---|---|
| 35,000 ft, outside air about −65 °F, about 500 mph | ISA standard atmosphere (−54 °C at 35,000 ft); typical airliner cruise ~Mach 0.78 |
| Cabin about 10.9 psi vs about 3.5 psi outside | ISA pressure at 35,000 ft is about 3.5 psi; typical cabin altitude about 8,000 ft is about 10.9 psi |
| About 7.4 psi difference | 10.9 − 3.5 (airliner design maximums are about 8 to 8.6 psi) |
| About 15,500 lb on the door | **My estimate:** 7.4 psi × about 2,100 sq in (30 × 70 in opening). Real doors and cabin settings vary, so treat it as order of magnitude |
| Door is a plug type, opens inward first | Plug-door design (larger than its frame); pressure difference seals it |
| Useful consciousness 15–30 s after rapid decompression at 35,000 ft (30–60 s if gradual) | FAA AC 61-107B, Fig. 2-3 |
| Alaska 1282 | NTSB: door plug separated Jan 5, 2024 at about 14,830 ft, bolts not installed, no fatalities |
| Masks drop above about 14,000 ft cabin altitude | Standard airliner design (cabin altitude trigger about 14,000 ft) |
| Dive to 10,000 ft | Standard emergency descent target (about 10,000 ft or lowest safe altitude) |

Illustrative (not data): the door failure at 33.4 s, mist, flying objects, speed of the cabin altitude climb, and the descent timing (time-compressed). The scene is labeled "Illustrative · not a real incident".
